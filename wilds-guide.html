The Wilds guide

The Wilds is the top-down adventure inside WHAT HAPPENED clicker. You walk a character around, fight with weapons you physically swing with the mouse, gather materials, craft your own gear and earn rubies. What you win can be sent home to the clicker as gems, Golden What Happeneds and lootboxes. Open it from the game's menu (⚔️ The Wilds). The in-game 📜 board in town has the same help in short form.

On a touch screen, on-screen buttons appear instead. In Settings, Assisted swings lets you click to swing automatically at 70% damage, which helps on trackpads and touch screens. Settings also has graphics, resolution and frame-rate options if the game runs slowly.

Fighting: swings, shields and parries

Your weapon follows the mouse. To hit hard you have to swing: move the mouse quickly across the target, like the real thing. A slow drag only bumps it. The swing meter under your hotbar shows how fast your blade is moving. Past the first mark it hurts; past the second it's a Perfect hit. Slashing back and forth builds a combo, and the tip of the blade hits harder than the hilt.

Shields block most hits from the front but cost stamina. If you run out, your guard breaks. Raise the shield just as a hit lands to parry: you take no damage, the attacker is stunned and projectiles fly back. You can't attack while blocking, and falling rocks and meteors can't be blocked.

Watch for red shapes on the ground: that's where an attack is about to land. Dash out of them.

Weapons

  • Swords: the all-rounder.
  • Axes turn slower but hit harder, and chop trees.
  • Pickaxes are weak in a fight but mine rocks and ores.
  • Daggers are quick and hit much harder from behind.
  • Spears: flick the mouse outward from your character to thrust, then pull back. A thrust hits harder the faster you are moving toward the target; dashing into it adds up to +90%.
  • Bows: press anywhere, pull the mouse away from where you want to shoot, and let go. Hold a full draw briefly for a steady shot. Bows need arrows.
  • Staffs (made at the Rune Altar) move like spears and hit weakly up close. In a Mystic's hands they cast spells: fire for single targets, lightning for groups, frost for a bit of both (every third frost hit slows). Hold the staff drawn back, cursor close to you, to charge a spell. Holding past full overcharges it for a little extra damage, but once the meter turns red the staff turns on you until you cast.

Town, zones and bosses

You start in Wildgate Town, which is safe: nobody can fight there. The town has the crafting stations, the Trader, four townsfolk who give quests, the Hall of Gods, the Arena and the Waystone. Eight zones lie beyond it, each tougher than the last. The first four are reached through the town's gates; the last four, the Far Reaches, through the Waystone south of the plaza. Each zone has a boss, and beating it the first time opens the next zone.

Each zone also has its own monsters, tougher elites, and a rival adventurer who fights with the same weapons you do. Mines are dark, the Ember Wastes have lava, and Frostveil has ice and blizzards.

Quests: talk to the townsfolk (E) for quests that pay rubies. Each has a short story, then a new daily quest every day. A ! over someone's head means a new quest; a ? means one is ready to hand in.

The Hall of Gods is the dark temple north of the plaza. Its six statues take offerings of rubies and materials (and boss trophies at some levels). Every level blesses your clicker game: more CPS, click power, crits, offline earnings, gems or Golden.

The Far Reaches

The four zones past the Waystone are built to take a good while longer than the first four:

  • Tougher monsters. Each zone's monsters are made for that zone's own gear: tier 6 Orichalcum on Saltspray Coast, tier 7 Adamant on Stormspire Heights, tier 8 Soulsteel in the Cinder Capital and tier 9 Starmetal on the Skyreach Isles. With the gear from the zone before, they hurt a lot more and take a lot longer to bring down.
  • Gear that takes work. Each zone's best ore needs the pickaxe from the zone before it, so you mine it with your old gear first. Its bars are alloys: ore, a raw material from the same zone and a monster part, smelted two at a time at the Forge. The recipes need more of everything, and their fees are higher.
  • Sealed lairs. Each boss sleeps behind a barrier held shut by ward stones spread around its zone: three on the coast and the heights, four in the capital and on the isles. Make the zone's Ward Key at the Rune Altar (it needs two of the zone elite's parts), press E at a ward to wake it, and beat the Ward Guardian that comes for you. The key is only used up if you win. Once every ward is broken, the lair stays open for good.
  • Hazards. Tide flats on the coast slow you down. On the heights, lightning strikes near you every few seconds (never at the camp). The capital is dark and its streets burn. On the isles, step off an island and you fall back to where you stood, a fifth of your health lighter; a dash can carry you over a narrow gap, and gusts of wind try to blow you off the edge.

Each zone has a camp with its own quest giver, whose story walks you through the wards, and the townsfolk have new quests for the Far Reaches too.

The Arena and the Proving Grounds

The Arena is the round building north-east of the plaza. Nothing is ever lost in the Arena: a knockout there drops nothing, and you get back up a moment later. Its lobby is safe.

  • The Pit, the sand circle in the middle: anyone standing in it can fight anyone else in it.
  • Duels: press E next to another player in the lobby and challenge them. You are both brought into one of the four rings for a best of three rounds. You can play for a ruby stake (100 to 5,000 rubies each): both pay it at the start, and the winner takes the pot; a draw gives it back.
  • Team battles: make a party of 2 or 3 (E next to a player, then Invite to party) and challenge another party to a 2v2 or 3v3. A round ends when a whole team is down.
  • The queue: Varro, the Arena Master, can put you in the queue for a duel, a 2v2 or a 3v3; a match starts on its own when there are enough players.

Matches are rated: winning raises your rating and losing lowers it, more when you beat someone rated above you. Leaving a match counts as a loss. Your best rating gives you a rank title:

Every fight earns Glory (with a daily limit for each kind of fight), and your first five wins of the day also pay rubies. Spend Glory at Varro's shop on titles shown under your name, potions, arrows, and once a week a Champion's Coffer: a Golden Chest for your game, counted in the week's lootbox allowance. Fighting the same opponents over and over stops paying rating and Glory after a few matches a day. The ranking board in the lobby shows the top 20; ratings are reported by each player's own game, so it's for fun.

The Proving Grounds, through the gate on the left of the lobby, are co-op: stand in the ready circle, press E, and hold the ring against waves of monsters, alone or with everyone else who is there. Every wave brings more, and the more players there are, the more monsters come and the tougher each one is. Waves march through the zones: five from Greenwood, then five from the Mines, and so on. Every fifth wave adds elites and every tenth a champion. Each wave cleared pays rubies and Glory, with a bonus the first time you reach waves 10, 20, 30 and 40. Knocked out, you get back up when your team clears the wave; if everyone is down, the run is over. A run starts from the furthest checkpoint (every five waves) that everyone there has reached. Monsters in the Proving Grounds drop nothing.

You start as a Warrior. The other classes unlock by completing a challenge and then paying rubies. You can change class in town.

Each class has a bonus and its own skill tree: three branches of abilities and perks, learned with rubies from the top of each branch down (some only after beating a boss). Equip up to three abilities on R Q C. An ability you equip outside town starts on its cooldown, so plan your loadout before you leave. The Mystic uses mana (the blue bar) for abilities and spells. It refills slowly, faster while you stand still, and every staff blow up close gives some back.

Gathering and crafting

Swing an axe at trees and a pickaxe at rocks and ores. Each zone has its own materials, and the best ores are deeper in. Hit the glowing crack for double progress and a chance of extra. Harder ores need better tools.

Smelt ore into bars at the Forge, cut planks and leather at the Workbench, then craft gear. Every piece is made with a short minigame: the better you play, the better its quality, from Crude up to Legendary (up to 1.75× the stats, plus bonus traits). Practice lets you learn the minigames for free. In your bag, hover over any material to see where it comes from.

Rubies and rewards for the clicker

Rubies are the money of the Wilds. You earn them from monsters, ruby veins, bosses, quests and the Trader, and spend them on crafting fees, supplies, classes, skills and the Hall of Gods.

The Wilds can also send treasure home to the clicker. It arrives the next time you open the game:

  • Gems and Golden What Happeneds from bosses, elites and ruby veins, and from the Trader's weekly exchange (2,500 rubies for 3 gems, 6,000 rubies for 1 Golden, once a week each).
  • Lootboxes crafted at the Rune Altar.

So that the Wilds stays a bonus rather than replacing the clicker, at most 5 gems and 1 Golden What Happened a day can come from it, and crafted lootboxes have a weekly allowance.

Rubies can go the other way too: trade Golden What Happeneds for rubies in the clicker's Divine menu (250 rubies each, up to 4 Golden a week), or redeem a rubies code in the clicker's Settings.

Playing online

Other players in the same zone appear live; the 🌐 button at the top shows how many. Press Enter to chat or send an emote. The swear filter is on unless you turn it off in the 🌐 menu.

  • Outside town, players can fight each other with the same swings, shields, parries and abilities. You're protected for a few seconds after you arrive somewhere. In the Arena, only in the Pit and in matches; never in the Proving Grounds.
  • A player knocked out by another drops part of what they carry. The winner gets first pick, then anyone can grab it.
  • Bosses are shared: everyone in the arena fights the same one, and everyone who did enough damage gets the reward. Other monsters are your own.
  • You can always play offline from the 🌐 menu.

Online play runs on a free plan with a limit on how many people can be connected at once. If it's busy, the 🌐 menu says the Wilds is full and you keep playing offline; try again later.

Getting knocked out

If you're knocked out, part of what you've carried since leaving town drops where you fell. Go back and pick it up. Rubies and gear are never lost.
