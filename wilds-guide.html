▶ Play The Wilds How to play the clicker FAQ

The Wilds guide

The Wilds is the top-down adventure inside WHAT HAPPENED clicker. You walk a character around, fight with weapons you physically swing with the mouse, gather materials, craft your own gear and earn rubies. What you win can be sent home to the clicker as gems, Golden What Happeneds and lootboxes. Open it from the game's menu (⚔️ The Wilds). The in-game 📜 board in town has the same help in short form.

On a touch screen, put your thumb down anywhere on the left side to move (a stick appears under it) and drag anywhere else to swing. The buttons by your right thumb dash, raise your shield, cast your abilities and drink a potion, and a gold E button shows up when there's something to use. Settings has bigger or smaller buttons and a left-handed layout. Assisted swings, also in Settings, lets you click to swing automatically at 70% damage, which helps on trackpads and touch screens. Settings also has graphics, resolution and frame-rate options if the game runs slowly.

Fighting: swings, shields and parries

Your weapon follows the mouse. To hit hard you have to swing: move the mouse quickly across the target, like the real thing. A slow drag only bumps it. The swing meter under your hotbar shows how fast your blade is moving. Past the first mark it hurts; past the second it's a Perfect hit. Slashing back and forth builds a combo, and the tip of the blade hits harder than the hilt.

Shields block most hits from the front but cost stamina. If you run out, your guard breaks. Raise the shield just as a hit lands to parry: you take no damage, the attacker is stunned and projectiles fly back. You can't attack while blocking, and falling rocks and meteors can't be blocked.

Watch for red shapes on the ground: that's where an attack is about to land. Dash out of them.

Weapons

  • Swords: the all-rounder. A fast swing still hits harder than a slow one, but a sword gains less from speed than other weapons, so steady swings are nearly as good as wild ones.
  • Axes turn slower but hit harder, and chop trees.
  • Pickaxes are weak in a fight but mine rocks and ores.
  • Daggers are quick and hit much harder from behind.
  • Spears: flick the mouse outward from your character to thrust, then pull back. A thrust hits harder the faster you are moving toward the target; dashing into it adds up to +90%.
  • Bows: press anywhere, pull the mouse away from where you want to shoot, and let go. Hold a full draw briefly for a steady shot. Bows need arrows.
  • Staffs (made at the Rune Altar) move like spears and hit weakly up close. In a Mystic's hands they cast spells: fire for single targets, lightning for groups, frost for a bit of both (every third frost hit slows). Hold the staff drawn back, cursor close to you, to charge a spell. Holding past full overcharges it for a little extra damage, but once the meter turns red the staff turns on you until you cast.

Town, zones and bosses

You start in Wildgate Town, which is safe: nobody can fight there. The town has the crafting stations, the Trader, four townsfolk who give quests, the Hall of Gods, the Arena and the Waystone. Eight zones lie beyond it, each tougher than the last. The first four are reached through the town's gates; the last four, the Far Reaches, through the Waystone south of the plaza. Each zone has a boss, and beating it the first time opens the next zone.

Each zone also has its own monsters, tougher elites, and a rival adventurer who fights with the same weapons you do. Mines are dark, the Ember Wastes have lava, and Frostveil has ice and blizzards.

Quests: talk to the townsfolk (E) for quests that pay rubies. Each has a short story, then a new daily quest every day. A ! over someone's head means a new quest; a ? means one is ready to hand in.

The Hall of Gods is the dark temple north of the plaza. Its six statues take offerings of rubies and materials (and boss trophies at some levels). Every level blesses your clicker game: more CPS, click power, crits, offline earnings, gems or Golden.

The Far Reaches

The four zones past the Waystone are built to take a good while longer than the first four:

  • Tougher monsters. Each zone's monsters are made for that zone's own gear: tier 6 Orichalcum on Saltspray Coast, tier 7 Adamant on Stormspire Heights, tier 8 Soulsteel in the Cinder Capital and tier 9 Starmetal on the Skyreach Isles. With the gear from the zone before, they hurt a lot more and take a lot longer to bring down.
  • Gear that takes work. Each zone's best ore needs the pickaxe from the zone before it, so you mine it with your old gear first. Its bars are alloys: ore, a raw material from the same zone and a monster part, smelted two at a time at the Forge. The recipes need more of everything, and their fees are higher.
  • Sealed lairs. Each boss sleeps behind a barrier held shut by ward stones spread around its zone: three on the coast and the heights, four in the capital and on the isles. Make the zone's Ward Key at the Rune Altar (it needs two of the zone elite's parts), press E at a ward to wake it, and beat the Ward Guardian that comes for you. The key is only used up if you win. Once every ward is broken, the lair stays open for good.
  • Hazards. Tide flats on the coast slow you down. On the heights, lightning strikes near you every few seconds (never at the camp). The capital is dark and its streets burn. On the isles, step off an island and you fall back to where you stood, a fifth of your health lighter; a dash can carry you over a narrow gap, and gusts of wind try to blow you off the edge.

Each zone has a camp with its own quest giver, whose story walks you through the wards, and the townsfolk have new quests for the Far Reaches too.

Boss attacks

Before almost every attack lands, the ground shows where: a red shape is about to be hit, so step or dash out of it. A dash makes you untouchable for a moment, so you can also dash through an attack. A raised shield stops blows and shots from in front, but not things falling from above, waves along the ground or the Shared Burden. Each boss uses only some of these, and more of them, more often, as its health drops (its bar says Enraged past two thirds and Furious past one third).

The Arena and the Proving Grounds

The Arena is the round building north-east of the plaza. Nothing is ever lost in the Arena: a knockout there drops nothing, and you get back up a moment later. Its lobby is safe.

  • The Pit, the sand circle in the middle: anyone standing in it can fight anyone else in it.
  • Duels: press E next to another player in the lobby and challenge them. You are both brought into one of the four rings for a best of three rounds. You can play for a ruby stake (100 to 5,000 rubies each): both pay it at the start, and the winner takes the pot; a draw gives it back.
  • Team battles: with a party of 2 or 3 in the Arena (see Playing online), challenge another party to a 2v2 or 3v3. A round ends when a whole team is down.
  • The queue: Varro, the Arena Master, can put you in the queue for a duel, a 2v2 or a 3v3; a match starts on its own when there are enough players.

Potions are limited in arena fights: at most 3 potions per match (a duel, a team battle or a tournament duel), per stay in the Pit (a new stay starts each time you step in or get back up) and per run of the Proving Grounds. The potion button shows how many you have left.

Matches are rated: winning raises your rating and losing lowers it, more when you beat someone rated above you. Leaving a match counts as a loss. Your best rating gives you a rank title.

Seasons: each calendar month (UTC) is a season with its own leaderboard. When a new one starts on the 1st, every rating moves halfway back to 1,000 (1,600 becomes 1,300), and the board starts empty. If you played at least 5 rated matches in a season, Varro has a reward for the best rank you reached in it: Glory, and from Gold up a title for that season, such as Gold · Nov '26. The Arena menu shows when the season ends.

Every fight earns Glory (with a daily limit for each kind of fight), and your first five wins of the day also pay rubies. Spend Glory at Varro's shop on titles shown under your name, potions, arrows, rubies, gems and Golden What Happened for your game (a few a week, and inside the Wilds' daily limit of 5 gems and 1 Golden), follower packs (they wait on the game's Followers page, to open free), once a week a Champion's Coffer (a Golden Chest for your game, counted in the week's lootbox allowance), and a Guild Chest for 15,000 Glory: one hero guild at random. Fighting the same opponents over and over stops paying rating and Glory after a few matches a day. The board in the lobby has two leaderboards, the highest ratings and the highest waves reached in the Proving Grounds, each with the top 20 and your own place; they are reported by each player's own game, so they're for fun.

Tournaments: anyone can open one from Varro's Tournaments tab. The host picks an entry fee (free up to 5,000 rubies), how many players (4, 8 or 16), what 2nd and 3rd place get (nothing up to 40% and 20% of the pot; the winner always gets the most) and whether everyone's gear is locked to one tier: then every player fights its duels with the same kit of that tier (their own kinds of weapons, plain armor and shield, no ring), and their own gear comes back afterwards. Everyone pays the fee when they sign up; the winner takes the pot, or what is left of it after 2nd and 3rd. It is a knockout of best-of-three duels, the best rated seeded apart; 3rd place, when it pays, is played for by the two semi-final losers. Stay in the Arena while it runs: your duels start on their own, and an opponent who doesn't turn up loses by walkover. A tournament that is called off, or one you didn't get into because it was full, gives your fee back.

The Proving Grounds, through the gate on the left of the lobby, are co-op: stand in the ready circle, press E, and hold the ring against waves of monsters, alone or with everyone else who is there. Every wave brings more, and the more players there are, the more monsters come and the tougher each one is. Waves march through the zones: five from Greenwood, then five from the Mines, and so on. Every fifth wave adds elites and every tenth a champion. Each wave cleared pays rubies and Glory, with a bonus the first time you reach waves 10, 20, 30 and 40. Knocked out, you get back up when your team clears the wave; if everyone is down, the run is over. Every run starts at wave 1, and you can drink at most 3 potions in a run. Monsters in the Proving Grounds drop nothing.

Hidden dungeons

Every zone hides a dungeon. Its way down looks like any other boulder until you have beaten that zone's boss; then it splits open and glows, and the camp's keeper will tell you roughly which way to look. Once you have found it, it shows on your map. Go down alone or with a party of up to 4: a party online shares one copy of the dungeon, and alone it is all yours (offline too). Nothing is lost in a dungeon.

  • Three rooms, then the boss, each room full of the zone's own monsters (the Sunken Vault, under the coast, is harder: the Flooded Hall has Saltspray and Stormspire monsters, the Broken Gallery the Cinder Capital's and the Sky Breach the Skyreach Isles', with a champion). Start a room from its ready circle. Once it starts, the room seals with everyone inside, and clearing it opens the way on. After the third room, the boss waits in its chamber.
  • Everything scales with your party: more and tougher monsters in the rooms, more health for the boss, and better odds of rare essences in the chests, for every player who comes along.
  • The Shared Burden: once a dungeon boss is down to two thirds of its health (the Drowned King from the start), it marks one player with a blue circle. The circle follows that player and shows a count; when the count reaches 0 it bursts, and its damage is split between everyone standing inside: two players take half each, four a quarter each. Alone you take all of it: most of your health, and in the Far Reaches' dungeons enough to knock you out even at full health with gear made for that zone. So with a party, gather in the marked player's circle; alone, dash just as it bursts (a dash makes you untouchable for a moment) and keep your health up in case you miss. Shields can't block it. The circle's label tells you what to do, and after the burst it shows how many ways the hit was split.
  • The frenzy: if a boss fight goes on for more than 6 minutes (7 in the Far Reaches' dungeons), the boss goes into a frenzy and hits 2.5 times as hard. A warning comes a minute before, and the boss bar counts the last minute down.
  • Wipes: if everyone goes down in a room, only that room starts again. Rooms you cleared stay cleared, even if the page reloads, until nobody has been in the dungeon for 30 minutes. Knocked out outside a room fight, you are back up at the entrance in a moment. You get 3 potions per room and 3 for the boss.
  • Rewards: each room pays rubies (up to 9 rooms a day, all dungeons together), and the first 6 rooms a day also open a chest of essences: rarer ones in deeper dungeons and later rooms, and sometimes the dungeon's own. Luck and a bigger party help. The room monsters drop nothing, so a dungeon is no way around the zones' materials. Each boss's first kill each week pays its Heart, rubies and sometimes a Crucible Catalyst; after that a few rubies until Monday.
  • The Sunken Vault's Drowned King also pays, the first time each week, 300 Glory, 3 Vault Seals and a Golden Chest for your game. Maren the Vault Keeper, in its entry hall, sells for Vault Seals: Max Potions, the titles Vaultbreaker and King's Bane, and once a week a Mythic Vault lootbox for your game (inside the weekly lootbox allowance).

Hero guilds

Every hero of the clicker, all 29 upgrades, has a guild, and the Wilds are the only place to get them. Guildmaster Aurelio stands just outside the Rune Altar with 6 guild charters on his table, a new random six every two days, at local midnight. Each charter is expensive (rubies and materials from its hero's zone, more for later heroes) and comes with a contract: take it, finish it (clear monsters, bring down elites or the zone's boss, or gather in that zone), then pay. You work on one contract at a time, and it stays yours even if the table changes. Once you buy a charter, that guild is sold out until the next six.

There is no limit on guilds: each one adds +25% to its hero's CPS in the clicker (for OP mouse, to the click power it gives), and they stack. The hero's button in the clicker takes on the guild's colours and crest, and grows grander with more guilds. The Arena's Glory shop also sells a Guild Chest (15,000 Glory) with one guild at random.

Gathering and crafting

Swing an axe at trees and a pickaxe at rocks and ores. Each zone has its own materials, and the best ores are deeper in. Hit the glowing crack for double progress and a chance of extra. Harder ores need better tools.

Smelt ore into bars at the Forge, cut planks and leather at the Workbench, then craft gear. Every piece is made with a short minigame: the better you play, the better its quality, from Crude up to Legendary (up to 1.75× the stats, plus bonus traits). Practice lets you learn the minigames for free. In your bag, hover over any material to see where it comes from.

Essences

Essences are rare materials for the Crucible. Every rock and ore vein you mine can hold one or two kinds, deeper stone rarer kinds; hitting the glowing crack and your luck raise the odds. The hidden dungeons' room chests give them too, and each dungeon has an essence of its own and a boss's Heart. Essences are always kept safe in town: never carried, never lost when you are knocked out, never sold. Each has a rarity, from Common to Mythic, and an aspect:

The Crucible: relics and gear for the clicker

The Crucible stands east of the Rune Altar. Put in 3 to 8 essences, pick Wilds gear (a random weapon or piece of armor at your own gear tier) or gear for the clicker, and play its minigame: pour each essence to its mark, catch each essence's rune, then seal it. You get one random relic. Its tier is rolled from the essences' rarity (most of all), how many you put in, your score, your luck, Resonance (every essence of one aspect), a Crucible Catalyst (from a dungeon boss: never Common) and your bad luck so far (each forge without a Legendary makes the next a little luckier). Its boosts follow the aspects you put in, and its colours are your essences' colours. Mythic and Primordial stay rare whatever you put in: at most 3% and 0.5% a forge.

  • Uniques: a Legendary or better forge can make one of the unique pieces, each with its own name, words, shape and outline, and a boost more. Most need that dungeon's Heart among the essences: the Cinder Crypt's for Wyrmtongue's wavy blade, the Sunken Vault's for the Tidecaller's Trident, and so on.

  • Upgrade: a relic can be raised to +5, each level adding 8% to its boosts, for essences of its rarity band and rubies.
  • Gear for the clicker waits in your Relic Satchel until you send it home. In the clicker, open Items › Gear and wear one piece in each of five slots: Cursor, Crown, Cloak, Ring and Relic. Three or five worn pieces sharing a main aspect are attuned (+10% or +25% to it). You can send a piece back to the Wilds to trade it.

Trading and the Auction Hall

Gear, relics and gear for the clicker can change hands. Each piece shows who forged it and how many times it has been traded, and every piece is rebuilt from its recipe when it arrives, so it can never be better than it really is. Worn gear and the starter kit stay with you.

  • Trade face to face: press E next to another player and choose Offer a trade. Each of you puts in up to 4 items and up to 100,000 rubies. Accept the deal, then confirm it; any change means accepting again. Once you have both confirmed the same deal it goes through on both sides. What you offer is set aside until then, and comes back if the trade is called off or runs out of time. Up to 10 trades a day.
  • The Auction Hall: talk to Ottoline the Auctioneer on the plaza. List up to 5 items at once with a starting price, an optional buy-now price and a length from 1 to 48 hours. Bids must beat the top bid by 5%; your rubies are set aside while you lead and come back if you are outbid. When it ends, the winner claims the piece and the seller is paid, less the Hall's 5%. A winner who doesn't claim within 3 days loses it back to the seller.
  • Both need online play. There is no server watching trades, so the game checks what it can, but a determined cheater could still copy a piece by restoring an old save, as with anything else in a game saved in your browser.

Rubies and rewards for the clicker

Rubies are the money of the Wilds. You earn them from monsters, ruby veins, bosses, quests and the Trader, and spend them on crafting fees, supplies, classes, skills and the Hall of Gods.

The Wilds can also send treasure home to the clicker. It arrives the next time you open the game:

  • Gems and Golden What Happeneds from bosses, elites and ruby veins, and from the Trader's weekly exchange (2,500 rubies for 3 gems, 6,000 rubies for 1 Golden, once a week each).
  • Lootboxes crafted at the Rune Altar.

So that the Wilds stays a bonus rather than replacing the clicker, at most 5 gems and 1 Golden What Happened a day can come from it, and crafted lootboxes have a weekly allowance.

Rubies can go the other way too: trade Golden What Happeneds for rubies in the clicker's Divine menu (250 rubies each, up to 4 Golden a week), or redeem a rubies code in the clicker's Settings.

The crew from the clicker

The characters from the clicker's upgrades don't stay in the factory. Once an upgrade has reached a certain level in the clicker (your best ever, so a prestige doesn't send them home), its character moves to Wildgate and sets up in the yard of the WH Depot, south-east of the plaza. The Depot itself lists who has arrived and who is still on the way, and the clicker marks their upgrade with 🏕️.

Each of them has a short story and a daily quest. Some steps are done in the clicker: get their upgrade to level 100, prestige, or reach a Renown level. Those count what the clicker has saved, so they move on while you play there, and the upgrade's button shows the quest's progress. The last step of each story also sends a boost to the clicker's Items, like an Hourglass or a Time Machine. The crew never send gems, Golden or lootboxes.

Renown: one rank for both games

Renown is a single rank made of everything you did in the clicker and in the Wilds. Both show the same number: the ⭐ in the clicker's top bar and the ⭐ next to your rubies here. Each level gives +1% CPS and click power in the clicker and +0.5% rubies from monsters, bosses and veins in the Wilds.

Points come only from lifetime progress, so a prestige never lowers your Renown. Every source has a cap and most grow slower and slower: one game on its own gets you to about Renown 70, and the top, Renown 100, needs both.

Playing online

Other players in the same zone appear live; the 🌐 button at the top shows how many. Press Enter to chat or send an emote. The swear filter is on unless you turn it off in the 🌐 menu.

  • Outside town, players can fight each other with the same swings, shields, parries and abilities. You're protected for a few seconds after you arrive somewhere. In the Arena, only in the Pit and in matches; never in the Proving Grounds.
  • A player knocked out by another drops part of what they carry. The winner gets first pick, then anyone can grab it.
  • Bosses are shared: everyone in the arena fights the same one, and everyone who did enough damage gets the reward. Other monsters are shared too: you see the same ones in the same places, a monster someone brings down falls for everyone, damage from several players adds up, and a monster one player has woken is where they have it for the others. Whoever fought a monster gets its loot.
  • Parties: walk up to another player and press E to invite them (up to 4 in a party). Party members can't hurt each other, see where each other are (top left of the screen) and have their own chat: start a line with /p, pick Party in the chat bar, or press P. A party stays together across zones and when you reload.
  • Every zone's camp is safe ground (the dashed ring): monsters ignore you there and nobody, monster or player, can hurt you. Hitting something from inside the ring takes the shelter away for a few seconds.
  • You can always play offline from the 🌐 menu.

Online play runs on a free plan with a limit on how many people can be connected at once. If it's busy, the 🌐 menu says the Wilds is full and you keep playing offline; try again later.

Getting knocked out

If you're knocked out, part of what you've carried since leaving town drops where you fell. Go back and pick it up. Rubies and gear are never lost.
